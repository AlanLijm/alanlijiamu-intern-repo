# 1.3 Research Assignment: Competitive Landscape — Focus Bear vs Other Productivity Apps
Apps Researched
Freedom — cross-device (Mac, Windows, iOS, Android, Chrome) website/app blocker with scheduled sessions, Locked Mode, and ambient sounds. Subscription-based (~$8.99/mo or $39.99/yr).
Cold Turkey — Windows/Mac-only desktop blocker known for its extremely hard-to-bypass "Frozen Turkey" mode, which can lock the entire computer. One-time purchase (~$39), no mobile support.
Tiimo — visual daily planner built specifically for ADHD/autistic/neurodivergent users, using color-coded icon timelines, gentle transition nudges, and an AI co-planner to break down tasks. Subscription-based, mobile + web.
Routinery — step-by-step guided routine app with voice-guided timers that walk users through a sequence of habits (e.g. morning routine) one at a time. Free with in-app purchases, mobile-focused.
What makes Focus Bear different from these apps?

Most competitors specialize in a single piece of the productivity puzzle: Freedom and Cold Turkey are pure distraction blockers with no habit or task structure; Tiimo is a visual day planner with no hard blocking; Routinery is a guided routine walker with no task prioritization or website blocking. Focus Bear tries to combine all three in one app — habit/routine building (like Routinery), task management with a priority-scoring algorithm, and distraction blocking with a Super Strict Mode (like Freedom/Cold Turkey) — under one roof, aimed specifically at people with ADHD.

If you were a user, why would you choose Focus Bear over competitors?

If I wanted a single app instead of stacking three separate subscriptions (a blocker + a planner + a habit tracker), Focus Bear's all-in-one approach is the main draw. For someone who specifically wants blocking and habit-building to work together — e.g. an evening routine that also blocks distracting apps during it — Focus Bear covers that in a way none of the four competitors do individually.

What's one feature that other apps have that Focus Bear doesn't?

Cold Turkey's Frozen Turkey mode is a good example: it can lock a user out of their entire computer, and once started, there is genuinely no way to cancel it early — not by uninstalling, restarting, or any other workaround. Based on my testing in the previous assignment, Focus Bear's "Super Strict Mode" claims similar unstoppable behavior, but in practice the stop button still works mid-session. Cold Turkey's lock is enforced at a much deeper technical level, which is a real gap compared to Focus Bear's current implementation.

Based on your research, what's one improvement Focus Bear could make?

Focus Bear could take a page from Tiimo's approach to visual time perception — Tiimo represents the day as a draining color timeline rather than plain text/numbers, which is specifically designed to help ADHD users who struggle to feel time passing. Focus Bear's habit countdown screen (as I noted in the previous testing assignment) currently just shows a name, a number, and a music button — adding a visual progress ring or timeline element, similar to Tiimo, would likely make the countdown experience feel less flat and more supportive for its target ADHD audience.
