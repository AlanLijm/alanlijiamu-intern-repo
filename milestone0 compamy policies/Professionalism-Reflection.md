# Professionalism at Focus Bear — Reflection

## Research & Learn

### What are examples of professional vs. unprofessional behaviour in a casual work environment?

**Professional behaviour:**

- Responding to messages within a reasonable time, even if just to say "got it, will follow up"
- Being on time (or giving notice) for meetings
- Giving credit to teammates, not taking sole credit for shared work
- Being honest about mistakes or blockers instead of hiding them
- Keeping jokes/humour light and inclusive — nothing that targets a person or group

**Unprofessional behaviour:**

- Ignoring messages for long periods without acknowledgement
- Repeatedly missing deadlines without communicating
- Making jokes that put down or exclude a teammate
- Blaming others instead of taking accountability
- Interrupting or dismissing others' ideas in meetings

### What does respectful communication look like in remote teams?

- Using a friendly, neutral tone in written messages — sarcasm and dry humour don't translate well over text, so being explicit helps avoid misunderstandings
- Assuming good intent when a message seems blunt, rather than reacting defensively
- Disagreeing with the idea, not the person (e.g. "I see it differently because..." rather than "that's wrong")
- Responding to feedback with curiosity ("could you give me an example?") instead of getting defensive

### How can you give and receive feedback professionally?

- Focus on specific actions or outcomes, not the person's character (e.g. "this function threw an error in testing" not "you're bad at this")
- Assume good intent — most feedback is meant to help, not attack
- Ask clarifying questions instead of reacting defensively ("Can you show me what you mean?")
- When giving feedback, be specific and constructive, and offer a suggestion where possible, not just criticism

### Review of "Suggestions for interns on working with supervisors"

- Interns are expected to become increasingly self-led over time — don't wait to be told what to do, but also don't go rogue. If unsure, say: "Based on my research, I think I should do ... next. Is that what you'd recommend?"
- Come to first/regular meetings prepared: read the brief, bring an agenda, and do a bit of homework before asking questions.
- Communication should be escalated based on urgency, from lightest to strongest: Email (reply within 48h) → Discord/Teams (within 24h) → SMS (within 2h) → Call (true emergency)
- Give at least one update at the end of each working day — don't wait to be chased.
- If stuck: search online/ask ChatGPT first → still stuck, ask a teammate → still blocked after 30 minutes → reach out to your supervisor.
- Avoid: waiting around for instructions, coming to meetings unprepared, not taking notes, asking questions that could be Googled in 10 seconds, going quiet without updates, ignoring repeated feedback, relying on only one communication channel for urgent issues, and vague calendar invite titles.

## Reflection

### Have you ever experienced or witnessed great teamwork and professionalism? What made it effective?

I have experienced good teamwork in group projects. What made it effective was that everyone respected each other's opinions, communicated openly about progress and blockers, and took ownership of their assigned tasks — trying their best to complete their part rather than leaving it to others. This mutual respect and accountability meant the team could rely on each other and work efficiently towards the shared goal.

### What steps can you take to make sure your communication is clear, respectful, and inclusive?

I will make sure my wording is friendly and respectful, avoiding language that could come across as blunt or dismissive, especially in text-based communication where tone can be easily misread. I will also take other people's perspectives into account when expressing my own ideas — for example, acknowledging their viewpoint before disagreeing, and being open to the possibility that I might be missing context they have.

### How can you help create a positive, fun, and professional work environment?

I will make an effort to attend every meeting and actively participate, rather than just being present. I will also try to build good relationships with my teammates by being approachable, friendly, and supportive — for example, being willing to help out when someone is stuck, and keeping a positive attitude even when things get busy or challenging.

### How can you prepare effectively for meetings with supervisors and colleagues?

I will read any project brief or relevant materials beforehand, bring a short agenda, and come with specific questions or blockers, rather than giving an impromptu speech without preparation.

### What would it look like to be proactive during your internship?

Being proactive means not waiting to be told what to do — if I'm unsure of the direction, I'll share my reasoning and proposed next step rather than staying idle. It also means giving regular updates instead of waiting to be chased.

### How might you follow up with colleagues or supervisors if you need information/action from them? How would you escalate it further if you don't get a response and when would you escalate?

I would start with the lightest-touch channel appropriate to the urgency — email for general, non-urgent items (expecting a reply within 48 hours), Discord/Teams for medium-priority items needing a reply within 24 hours, SMS for urgent matters needing a response within 2 hours, and a call only for true emergencies. If I don't get a response within the expected window for something time-sensitive, I would escalate to a more direct channel rather than waiting indefinitely.

### What are some things you won't do?

- I won't sit idle waiting for instructions without attempting to move forward or ask
- I won't come to meetings unprepared or without having reviewed relevant material
- I won't ask questions I could quickly answer myself via a search
- I won't go quiet on a task without giving updates
- I won't ignore repeated feedback without reflecting on it
