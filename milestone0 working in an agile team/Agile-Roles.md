Roles in an Agile Team
Research & Learn
What are the key roles in an Agile team, and what does each role do?

Developers Design, build, and test the actual product — writing code, fixing bugs, reviewing peers' pull requests, and turning backlog items into working software. Responsible for the technical "how."

QA (Quality Assurance) Verifies that what's been built actually works and meets requirements — through manual testing, automated test suites, and exploratory testing. Catches defects before they reach users and helps define what "done" means from a quality standpoint. In smaller or highly automated teams, QA responsibilities may be shared by developers rather than a dedicated role.

Product Manager (PM) Owns the "what" and "why" — decides what gets built based on user needs, business goals, and data. Prioritizes the backlog, writes/clarifies requirements, and is the main point of contact for stakeholders on product direction. In Scrum this maps closely to the Product Owner.

Agile Project Manager (Agile PM) Owns the "how the team works" — facilitates ceremonies (stand-ups, retros, planning), removes blockers, tracks progress, and protects the team's process from disruption. In Scrum this maps to the Scrum Master; in Kanban the role is lighter-weight and more about coaching flow than running fixed ceremonies.

Other supporting roles (e.g., stakeholders, UX, customer support)

Stakeholders: people outside the core team (executives, clients, other departments) with an interest in the outcome; provide input and feedback but don't do the day-to-day work.
UX/UI Designers: shape how the product looks and feels, conduct user research, and hand off designs/prototypes for developers to build.
Customer Support: the feedback loop from real users back into the team — surfaces bugs, pain points, and feature requests based on what users actually experience.
How do these roles collaborate in Kanban vs. Scrum?

Structure

Scrum: Fixed roles (Product Owner, Scrum Master, Dev Team) with clearly separated responsibilities
Kanban: Roles are more fluid; often no formal Scrum Master — a team lead or PM may absorb facilitation duties

Collaboration rhythm

Scrum: Structured around sprint ceremonies — the whole team plans, reviews, and retros together on a fixed cadence
Kanban: Continuous — roles collaborate as work flows across the board, with sync points (e.g. daily stand-up) rather than sprint boundaries

Prioritization

Scrum: PM/Product Owner sets the sprint backlog upfront; team commits to it for the sprint
Kanban: PM continuously reprioritizes the backlog; developers pull the next-highest-priority item whenever they have capacity

QA involvement

Scrum: Often tied to sprint boundaries — testing happens within or right after the sprint
Kanban: QA is typically continuous, embedded directly in the flow (e.g. a "Ready for QA" column)

Reflection

What is your role, and how does it contribute to the Agile process?

I am a backend developer intern at Focus Bear. My role is to write code and fix bugs, working through the onboarding issues in the repository. This contributes to the Agile process by turning backlog items into working, reviewable code — each issue I complete and move through the Kanban board (from In Progress to Ready for Review to Approved) keeps the team's overall progress visible and moving forward.

How do your responsibilities overlap or interact with other roles in the team?

My responsibilities overlap with several other roles. QA tests the code I write and reports back any bugs or issues, which I then need to fix. The PM defines and prioritizes what I need to work on through the onboarding issues, giving me clear requirements to follow. The Agile PM facilitates the team's process — for example, running stand-ups and removing blockers — which helps keep my individual work aligned with the rest of the team.

How my role fits into the Agile team: 
As a backend developer at Focus Bear, my role fits into the Agile team by turning tasks the PM assigns into working code. I improve my code quality based on any feedback QA gives me, and I try my best to meet the requirements the PM sets out. I also coordinate with the Agile PM by keeping them updated on my progress, so the team always has visibility into what I'm working on.

One role I frequently collaborate with, and how I can improve teamwork with them: 
The role I frequently collaborate with is the PM. They tell me what I need to achieve through the onboarding issues. Through the Kanban board, they can see what I'm currently working on, what I've completed, which issues I'm blocked on, and which ones I haven't started yet.

One way I can improve teamwork with the PM is to update my issue status more promptly and add a comment whenever I get blocked, instead of just leaving it silently in "In Progress" — this way the PM can see the blocker immediately rather than only noticing it during a status check.
